Single-block marble bathtubs: a guide for architects and designers

A solid stone bathtub is often the most expensive object in a bathroom, and the heaviest. Here is what to know before you specify one for a luxury residence, villa or hotel suite.

By Sunrise Natural Stones, Udaipur. 4 October 2026

A single-block bathtub is hollowed out of one solid piece of marble. Nothing is glued or assembled, so the veining wraps unbroken around the tub. That is why designers of ultra-luxury homes specify them, and why they cost what they do.

What "single block" means

Many stone tubs on the market are built from slabs bonded at the corners, or cast from crushed stone and resin. A single-block tub starts as one quarried block, larger than the finished tub on every side. The outside is shaped first, then the basin is cut away until the walls reach their final thickness.

Because the tub is one piece, the block must be sound all the way through. A crack hidden in the middle of a block can ruin weeks of work, so selection at the quarry matters more than anything that happens in the workshop.

Denser stones tolerate daily water contact best. Very soft or heavily veined marbles look spectacular but need more care, and veins can be lines of weakness in a thin wall. We advise on each block, not just the variety.

Size and weight

Weight is the governing constraint. Marble weighs about 2.7 tonnes per cubic metre, so even a hollowed tub is heavy. A freestanding tub around 170 cm long often weighs 0.8 to 1.5 tonnes empty. Filled with water and a bather, add another 300 to 450 kg.

  • Floor loading: have a structural engineer confirm the floor can take the point load, especially on upper floors and in timber-framed houses.
  • Access route: check door widths, stairs, lifts and corners from the street to the bathroom. Many projects place the tub before walls or glazing are closed, or lift it in by crane.
  • Plumbing: fix the waste position and the type of overflow early. The drain is carved into the stone, so it cannot be moved later.

Shape, walls and finish

Oval and rounded forms are the most common, because their curves spread stress evenly. Rectangular and tapered forms are possible from suitable blocks. Wall thickness is a balance: thinner walls look refined and weigh less; thicker walls are stronger and hold heat longer.

Finishes are usually honed inside, which is less slippery, and polished or honed outside. Some designers choose a textured or hand-chiselled exterior to contrast with a smooth interior.

Care and longevity

Marble is calcium carbonate, so acids etch it. Use pH-neutral cleaners only, rinse after bathing and re-seal the stone periodically. Avoid bath products containing citric acid. Looked after properly, a solid stone tub lasts for generations, and minor wear can be honed out.

What drives the price

The finished tub may be less than half the volume of the block it came from. The rest becomes offcuts. Price therefore depends on:

  • The size and rarity of a sound block in the chosen stone
  • The complexity of the form and how thin the walls are
  • Hours of carving and hand finishing
  • Crating and freight for a single heavy piece

Questions buyers ask

Frequently asked questions

Is a single-block marble bathtub really carved from one piece?

Yes. The tub is hollowed out of one block of marble. There are no joints, adhesives or bonded sections, so the veining runs unbroken around the outside and through the basin.

How heavy is a solid marble bathtub?

Marble weighs about 2.7 tonnes per cubic metre. A freestanding tub around 170 cm long often weighs between 0.8 and 1.5 tonnes empty, depending on the stone, the shape and the wall thickness. Filled with water and a bather, add roughly another 300 to 450 kg. We give the exact figure with the quotation.

Does a stone bathtub keep water warm?

Yes. Thick stone has a lot of thermal mass. Once the stone has warmed up, it holds the water's heat longer than thin acrylic or steel.

How do you clean and maintain a marble bathtub?

Use pH-neutral cleaners only and rinse after use. Re-seal the stone periodically as your installer advises. Acidic products, bath bombs with citric acid and abrasive pads will etch or dull marble.

Can you carve a tub to an architect's drawing?

Yes. Send dimensioned drawings, and we will confirm what can be achieved from the blocks available in your chosen stone before you commit.
