How to import granite and marble from India

A practical, step-by-step guide for importers, fabricators and builders buying Indian stone for the first time.

By Sunrise Natural Stones, Udaipur. 4 October 2026

India is one of the world's largest producers of granite and marble. Buying direct from an Indian exporter can cut costs and widen your range. The process is simple once you know the steps.

1. Define what you need

Start with the stone, the format (blocks, full slabs or cut-to-size), the thickness, the finish, the quantity and your destination port. The clearer the brief, the more accurate the quotation.

2. Samples and photographs

Ask for samples to check colour and finish. Natural stone varies, so for slabs and blocks ask for photos and video of the actual material you will receive, numbered against the packing list.

3. Agree the Incoterm

TermSeller paysBuyer pays
FOB (Free on Board)Loading on the vessel at the Indian portOcean freight, insurance, import clearance, delivery
CFR (Cost and Freight)Loading plus ocean freight to your portInsurance, import clearance, delivery
CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)Loading, freight and minimum insurance to your portImport clearance, delivery

Under all three terms, risk passes to the buyer once the goods are on board in India. Many buyers take CIF for their first order and switch to FOB once they have a forwarder they trust.

4. Payment and production

Common arrangements in the stone trade are an advance payment with the balance against a copy of the bill of lading, or a letter of credit for larger orders. Lead time depends on the product: full slabs from stock ship fastest, while cut-to-size work and carved pieces take longer.

5. Inspection and packing

Before packing, ask for a final set of photos covering thickness, finish, edges and any defects. Stone should travel in ISPM-15 treated timber crates or A-frames, braced so nothing can shift in the container.

6. Documents

A standard export set is the commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, fumigation certificate and bill of lading. Your customs broker or forwarder uses these to clear the goods.

7. Customs by market

MarketWhat to know
USAA customs broker files the entry; ISF (10+2) data must be filed before loading. Duty rates on Indian goods have changed since 2025, so confirm with your broker.
United KingdomEORI number and customs declaration, usually through your forwarder.
European UnionEORI number and import declaration; CE marking and a declaration of performance depending on the product's end use.
AustraliaISPM-15 packaging and biosecurity inspection at the department's discretion. Engineered stone has been banned since 1 July 2024; natural stone is permitted.
New ZealandMPI biosecurity rules for timber packaging and container cleanliness.

This guide is general information, not customs or legal advice. Rules change, so confirm the current requirements with your broker before you ship.

8. Receiving the shipment

Unload carefully with suitable equipment. Check the bundles against the packing list, and photograph any damage before signing for delivery.

Questions buyers ask

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between FOB, CFR and CIF?

Under FOB the seller loads the goods on the ship at the Indian port and the buyer pays ocean freight and insurance. Under CFR the seller also pays freight to your port. Under CIF the seller pays freight and minimum insurance. Under all three, risk passes to the buyer once the goods are on board in India.

Can I order less than a full container?

Yes. Through your freight forwarder, smaller orders can travel as shared-container (LCL) cargo. It costs more per tonne, but works for single projects or special pieces such as bathtubs.

How do I check quality before the stone leaves India?

Ask for photographs and video of the actual slabs or blocks, numbered against the packing list. You can also visit or appoint an inspection agent.
