How to choose a stone supplier in India: a buyer's checklist

India has thousands of stone businesses, from quarry owners to traders. Here is how to tell which ones will deliver what you ordered.

By Sunrise Natural Stones, Udaipur. 4 October 2026

The right supplier matters more than the right price. Natural stone varies block to block, so a cheap quote is worthless if the container that arrives doesn't match the samples.

We are a stone supplier ourselves, so read this as a checklist from the trade, and apply it to us too.

1. Check they are a real, established business

  • A full business address you could visit, ideally near the quarries (most Indian marble and sandstone comes from Rajasthan).
  • Membership of export bodies such as FIEO, or registration with CAPEXIL for stone exporters.
  • Named people you can speak to, with company email addresses.
  • Years in business and countries shipped to, which you can confirm with references.

2. Ask for the real material, not catalogue photos

Good suppliers send photos and video of the actual slabs or blocks you will receive, numbered against the packing list. For blocks, ask for wet photos of all six faces. Be wary of anyone who only shares glossy catalogue images.

3. Get samples, then compare against the shipment

Samples show colour and finish, but stone varies. Ask how the supplier keeps a container consistent: slabs from one block kept together, and one quarry face for a large order.

4. Read the quotation closely

  • The Incoterm (FOB, CFR or CIF) and the named port.
  • Exact thickness, finish, sizes and tolerance.
  • Packing: ISPM-15 treated timber crates or A-frames.
  • Lead time, and what happens if it slips.

5. Agree payment terms that protect both sides

An advance with the balance against a copy of the bill of lading is common. For large orders, a letter of credit or a third-party inspection before loading adds security.

6. Check they can handle export paperwork

You will need a commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, fumigation certificate and bill of lading. An experienced exporter prepares these routinely and knows your country's requirements.

7. Start with one container

Even with a good supplier, start with one order, inspect it on arrival and build from there.

Questions buyers ask

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if an Indian stone supplier is genuine?

Check for a registered business address you can visit, export memberships such as FIEO, a track record of shipments to your country, and clear written quotations. Ask for photos and video of the actual material, not catalogue images.

Should I buy from a quarry owner or an exporter?

Quarry owners know their own stone deeply but usually offer only that stone. Exporters work across many quarries, so they can supply a wider range, mix stones in one container and handle shipping and paperwork.

What payment terms are normal with Indian stone suppliers?

An advance with the balance against a copy of the bill of lading is common, as is a letter of credit for larger orders. Agree terms in writing before production starts.
